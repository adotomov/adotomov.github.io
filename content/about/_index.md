title: "About Me"
---

I'm a 90s kid who got his first computer in 2000, and it didn't take long for it to take over my life. It started with the hardware — my parents bought me a book on PC internals, and I'd open the case in secret with a torch to study each component one by one, terrified they'd catch me because computers were expensive back then.

By high school I'd moved on to software. I picked up micro-controller programming, signed up for a software development club, and worked my way through Java, Visual Basic, PHP, and JavaScript. I installed my first Linux distro in 2006 and deployed my first LAMP stack the same year. I entered competitions and won a special prize for an interactive physics calculator I built. For a few years there, I spent every hour I could in front of a screen.

Then, around 2007, I talked myself out of it. I decided software wasn't a *serious* career and went off to study business, convinced I'd become a businessman and make a fortune — young and a little foolish, as these things go.

The detour turned out to matter. I moved to the Netherlands to study on my own in a foreign country, which taught me how to be responsible for myself while getting through a genuinely hard degree. And the whole time, I kept drifting back to code — experimenting with new stacks, learning new languages, unable to leave it alone. When I graduated and moved back to Bulgaria, I finally admitted the obvious: I wanted to work with computers.

Breaking in without a computer science degree wasn't easy — you have to earn the chance to prove yourself. But one thing led to another, my career took off, and here I am: 8+ years in, still in love with the craft. That winding path is also my edge. Having spent real time on the business side, I'm comfortable sitting between stakeholders and systems, turning fuzzy business problems into distributed systems that actually hold up.
